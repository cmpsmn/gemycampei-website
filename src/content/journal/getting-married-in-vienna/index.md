Vienna is made for weddings: baroque palaces, Gothic churches, grand hotels, coffee houses and vineyards on the edge of the city, all within a few minutes of each other. I live here and photograph weddings of every size, from a civil ceremony for two to a palace celebration with guests from around the world. This guide explains how getting married in Vienna works, and how to plan a day that feels like you.

## The civil ceremony: the legal part

In Austria, **only the civil wedding is legally binding**. It takes place at a registry office (*Standesamt*), and a church or symbolic ceremony can follow on the same day or later.

Before the wedding, the registry office checks that you are free to marry (the *Ermittlung der Ehefähigkeit*). You usually need passports or ID cards and birth certificates, and foreign citizens often need further documents, with certified translations. The requirements depend on your nationalities, so contact a Vienna registry office early and ask for your exact list.

The ceremony itself is short and personal: around 20 to 30 minutes, with your closest people around you. Many couples marry at one of Vienna's registry offices; by arrangement, civil ceremonies can also take place at certain other locations, such as palaces. An intimate civil wedding followed by a walk through the old town and a long dinner is one of my favourite days to photograph.

## Church and symbolic ceremonies

Vienna has some of the most beautiful churches in Europe. [Maria am Gestade](/vienna-wedding-photographer/maria-am-gestade-wedding-vienna/), one of the oldest Gothic churches in the city, is intimate and full of soft, coloured light. For a church wedding, contact the parish early, as popular churches are booked well ahead.

A **symbolic ceremony** (*freie Trauung*) with a celebrant can take place almost anywhere: in a palace garden, a vineyard or a castle park.

## Wedding venues in and around Vienna

- **City palaces.** Baroque halls, painted ceilings and courtyards in the heart of the city, like [Palais Daun-Kinsky](/vienna-wedding-photographer/palais-daun-kinsky-wedding-vienna/) on the Freyung.
- **Grand hotels and ballrooms.** Classic Viennese elegance for a large celebration.
- **Heurigen and vineyards.** Relaxed wine taverns on the edge of the city, with views over Vienna at sunset.
- **Castles in the countryside.** Less than an hour from the city, like [Schloss Hernstein](/vienna-wedding-photographer/schloss-hernstein-wedding-austria/) in Lower Austria, with a lake, a park and historic halls.

## When to get married in Vienna

**May, June and September** are my favourite months: mild, green and with long evenings. July and August are warm and lively; plan shade and water for your guests. **December** brings Christmas markets and festive lights for a winter wedding with cosy indoor venues. Golden hour in summer comes late, so a timeline with portraits after dinner often gives the most beautiful light.

## Photo spots in the old town

A short walk after the ceremony gives you timeless photos without a long drive: the narrow lanes and courtyards of the first district, the squares around the Hofburg, the Ringstraße and its parks, and staircases in palaces and hotels. Some famous gardens and palaces have rules or permits for professional photo shoots, so I check them before your day.
