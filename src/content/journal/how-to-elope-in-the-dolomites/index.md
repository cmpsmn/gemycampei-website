An elopement in the Dolomites is one of the most beautiful ways to get married: just the two of you, or a handful of your closest people, surrounded by some of the most dramatic mountains in the world. I grew up between these peaks, and I photograph elopements, proposals and couple sessions here every season. This guide answers the questions couples ask me most, in the order you will need them.

## Legal wedding or symbolic ceremony?

The first decision shapes everything else.

**A legal civil wedding in Italy** is possible for foreign couples, but the paperwork is complex: documents from your home country, translations and appointments at the town hall, and the requirements differ by nationality. For an easier start, I recommend marrying legally in your home town and celebrating your vows in the mountains.

**A symbolic ceremony** has no legal effect, and that is exactly its freedom. You can marry legally at home, before or after, and then exchange vows on a mountain meadow, beside a lake or on a quiet pass, at sunrise, with a celebrant, a friend or no one at all reading your words. Most of the couples I photograph choose this way. It keeps the day about the two of you, not about forms and opening hours.

## Choosing the place

The Dolomites stretch across South Tyrol, Trentino and the province of Belluno, and every valley has its own character. When we choose your place, I look at four things:

- **Access.** Can you reach it in a wedding dress? Many breathtaking views are a few minutes from a road or a lift. I keep walks to an easy hour at most.
- **Crowds.** Famous places like [Lago di Braies](/dolomites-elopement-photographer/lago-di-braies-elopement-s-and-j/) are magical at dawn and busy by mid-morning.
- **Light.** East-facing spots glow at sunrise, west-facing passes at sunset.
- **A backup.** A second place that works in cloud, rain or wind.

My favourites for elopements are quiet meadows above [La Val](/dolomites-elopement-photographer/la-val-elopement-r-and-d/) in Val Badia, the passes like [Passo delle Erbe](/dolomites-elopement-photographer/passo-delle-erbe-proposal-t-and-k/) and [Passo Gardena](/dolomites-elopement-photographer/passo-gardena-couple-session-k-and-b/), and alpine lakes at first light. You will find more in my guide to [the best elopement locations in the Dolomites](/journal/best-elopement-locations-in-the-dolomites/).

## When to elope

My favourite windows are **June to mid-July**, when wildflowers cover the meadows and the lakes have thawed, and **late September to mid-October**, when the larches turn gold and the air is crisp and clear. August is the busiest month and afternoon thunderstorms are common. From November to April many lifts and huts close, unless you dream of snow. Read more in [the best time to elope in the Dolomites](/journal/best-time-to-elope-in-the-dolomites/).

Whatever the month, **plan around sunrise**. The famous places are empty, the light is soft and the mountains turn pink. Sunset is the second-best option.

## Permits, rules and respect

For a small private ceremony and photos in public nature, you usually need no permit. But a few things are worth knowing:

- Some well-known spots are on **private land** or have introduced access fees or turnstiles.
- **Road access** is limited at some places in high summer, for example the road to Lago di Braies during the day and the road up to the Alpe di Siusi.
- **Leave no trace**: no confetti, no rice, no flower petals left behind, and stay on the paths in fragile meadows. Never walk into a meadow before it has been mown: the tall grass is the farmers' hay.

I check the current rules for your chosen place before your day.

## Weather and a plan B

Mountain weather changes quickly, and that is part of its magic: mist over a lake and dramatic clouds make some of the most beautiful photos. I always plan a backup place and, if possible, a flexible date or time. On the day, we decide together, early enough to keep everything relaxed.

## What to wear and bring

- Comfortable shoes under your dress, and your wedding shoes in a bag.
- A warm layer: even summer mornings are cold at 2,000 metres.
- A bag for your dress or suit if we change at the location.
- Your rings, your vows, water and a snack.
- Passports or ID, and an international driving licence if you rent a car.
