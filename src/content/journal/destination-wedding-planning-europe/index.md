A destination wedding is more than a wedding day. It is a few days with the people you love most, somewhere that means something to you: a castle an hour from Vienna, a countryside house in Sweden, a palace in Marrakech. I photograph weddings like these across Europe and beyond, and the best ones have one thing in common: good planning that leaves room for the unplanned moments. This guide shares what I have learned.

## 12 to 18 months ahead: the foundations

- **Choose the country and the venue.** Visit if you can. Think about travel for your guests: airports, trains and how long the journey takes.
- **Set the dates and the shape of the weekend.** Most destination weddings last two or three days: a welcome evening, the wedding day and a relaxed brunch or excursion afterwards.
- **Check the legal side.** Marrying legally abroad is often possible, but the paperwork depends on the country and your nationalities, and can take months. Many couples marry legally at home and hold a symbolic ceremony at their destination.
- **Consider a local planner.** Someone who speaks the language, knows the vendors and handles permits is worth a lot, especially for larger weddings.

## 8 to 12 months ahead: your team

Book your photographer and other key vendors. For photography, think about which moments matter to you across the whole weekend, not only the ceremony. The welcome dinner is often where the most relaxed, emotional photos happen: guests arriving, reunions, speeches without pressure. At [Leonie and Yassine's two-day wedding in Marrakech](/vienna-wedding-photographer/ksar-char-bagh-wedding-morocco/), the moonlit welcome dinner set the tone for everything that followed.

## Photography across a wedding weekend

- **Welcome evening:** reunions, candlelight, the first toasts.
- **Getting ready:** calm moments with family and friends, details, letters.
- **Ceremony and portraits:** planned around the best light of the day.
- **Traditions:** if your families bring different cultures, tell me about the rituals. At [Kyo and Benedikt's castle wedding](/vienna-wedding-photographer/schloss-hernstein-wedding-austria/), a Korean ceremony and a hanbok change were among the most beautiful moments.
- **The party** and **the morning after:** relaxed portraits in the destination, when all the pressure is gone.

My [Ultimate 3 Day collection](/wedding-photography-packages/) is designed for weddings like these, and 35mm film and Super 8 add a nostalgic layer to the whole weekend.

## 4 to 6 months ahead: guests and details

- Send invitations with clear travel information, a small website helps.
- Plan transport between the venues, especially on the wedding day.
- Think about the climate: shade and water in the south in summer, warm layers in the north and in the mountains.
- Plan a weather backup for every outdoor moment.

## The weeks before

Share a timeline with all vendors, including sunset time and a rain plan. A short call with your photographer a few weeks before the wedding, to go through the timeline, the family photo list and any traditions, means that on the day you can simply be present.

## Why I love destination weddings

Every destination wedding brings people together from far away. At [Araya and Christoffer's countryside wedding near Falun](/vienna-wedding-photographer/falun-wedding-sweden/), family and friends came together from far away to celebrate a love that bridged worlds.
